📚 article 21

How did the Supreme Court's interpretation of 'procedure established by law' in Article 21 evolve from the A.K. Gopalan case to the Maneka Gandhi case?
In *A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras* (1950), the Court held a narrow view, stating 'procedure established by law' simply meant a procedure enacted by a legislature. However, in *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978), this was overruled. The Court ruled that the procedure must be 'right, just, and fair,' not arbitrary or oppressive, effectively introducing the principles of natural justice and aspects of 'due process of law' into Article 21.
