📚 article 21

How did the Supreme Court's interpretation of 'procedure established by law' in Article 21 evolve from the A.K. Gopalan case to the Maneka Gandhi case?
In *A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras* (1950), the Court held that 'procedure established by law' meant any procedure laid down by a statute, even if arbitrary. This was overturned in *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978), where the Court ruled that the procedure must be 'right, just, and fair,' effectively incorporating the principle of 'due process of law' into Article 21.

Article 21's 'right to life' has been interpreted to include several unenumerated rights. Name at least three such rights established through judicial interpretation and cite one relevant landmark case.
The 'right to life' under Article 21 has been expanded to mean a life with human dignity. Key unenumerated rights include: 1. **Right to Privacy** (*K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India*), 2. **Right to a Clean Environment** (*M.C. Mehta cases*), and 3. **Right to Livelihood** (*Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation*). Other rights include the right to health and speedy trial.
